Research

We asked 1,000 people.

In early 2026 we asked 500 neurodivergent adults and 500 non neurodivergent adults in the UK about clothes. What irritates, what helps, and whether the fashion industry is paying attention.

The headline numbers went out in a press release. This page is the rest of it. Every question as it was asked, every answer as it was given.

Humaneight makes sensory sensitive clothing. We ran this study because we wanted to know if what we hear from our own community holds up at scale. It does.

Where the groups differ

Two things we noticed.

Among neurodivergent respondents, women report a harder time than men. 53% of women find it difficult to find clothes that meet their sensory needs, against 40% of men. 53% of women say brand labels communicate sensory information poorly or not at all, against 39% of men.

The youngest are the most forgiving of the industry. 68% of 18 to 24 year olds think designers and labels cater well to neurodivergent people, at least in some cases. Among 55 to 64 year olds it is 34%.

These are observations, not conclusions. The subgroups are small (60 people aged 18 to 24, 50 aged 55 to 64) and we have not tested them for statistical significance.

How the research was done.

Online survey of 1,000 UK adults aged 18 and over, completed 30 January to 6 February 2026. 500 identified as neurodivergent and 500 did not. Neurodivergence was self reported. Fieldwork by 3Gem Research and Insights on their in-house panel of double opt-in respondents, compliant with the Market Research Society code of conduct. Percentages are rounded, so some sets do not add to exactly 100.

The two groups differ in age. The neurodivergent group has a mean age of 39, the non neurodivergent group 51, and the non neurodivergent group has almost no one under 25. Comparisons between the groups should be read with that in mind.
